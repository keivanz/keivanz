# **1\. The Mindset Shift (The Transition)**

The transition from a successful Individual Contributor (IC) to a people manager requires a fundamental restructuring of how you define success, spend your time, and interact with your team.

### **The Core Shifts of Leadership**

* I do $\rightarrow$ We do: Your output is no longer your individual work; it is the collective throughput of your team.
* Speed $\rightarrow$ Clarity: Moving fast is useless if the team is marching in the wrong direction. Prioritize clear expectations over rapid, uncoordinated execution.
* Answers $\rightarrow$ Questions: Stop being the chief problem solver. Shift to asking powerful questions that coach your team to find the solutions themselves.
* Tear down $\rightarrow$ Coach up: Shift from critiquing mistakes to using them as developmental milestones.
* Confidence $\rightarrow$ Vulnerability: Building trust requires admitting when you don't know the answer.
* Taking bows $\rightarrow$ Giving credit: Your success is reflected in your team’s accomplishments. Shine the spotlight on them.
* Winning a race $\rightarrow$ Lifting a team: Transition from individual competition to collective enablement.

### **The Three Roles of a Manager**

Every leader must balance three primary areas of responsibility:

1. Momentum: Keeping the team moving forward, removing roadblocks, and ensuring steady, incremental progress.
2. Vision & Transformation: Painting a clear picture of the future, aligning it with company strategy, and driving meaningful change.
3. Growing People (The Emotional Dampener): Fostering long-term career growth, building self-awareness, and acting as an emotional buffer—absorbing organizational chaos and radiating calm, grounded clarity.

### **Operational Adjustments: From-To Matrix**

To operate effectively, leaders must consciously shift their daily beliefs and actions:

| Capability Area | From (The Tactical Operator) | To (The Strategic Leader) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Living in the Future | • I focus heavily on delivering immediate quarterly OKRs. • I wait for upper leadership to define the strategy. • I have no time for vision; I’m just trying to keep up. | • I balance immediate execution with future capability. • I actively get "on the balcony" to see the big picture. • I build the bench now to unlock future team growth. |
| Ruthless Prioritizing | • I try to monitor every single detail of my team's work. • I let others own my calendar and meeting schedule. • I find it extremely hard to say "No" to new requests. | • I shift focus from solving immediate problems to solving recurring patterns. • I am the absolute owner of my calendar and say "No" strategically. • I focus strictly on the unique work that only I can do. |
| Getting Done Through Others | • If something goes wrong, I feel I should have done it myself. • I worry that others won't do the work the way I would. • I prefer to remain in tight control of execution. | • My role is to enable, trust, and guide without micromanaging. • I tolerate discomfort and constructive mistakes as learning opportunities. • I scale our impact by putting heads together across teams. |
| Focusing on Outcomes | • Output-oriented: I focus on tracking tasks completed, lines of code, or hours worked. | • Outcome-oriented: I treat my team like a business, tracking metrics that prove real business value. |

### **Polarity Management: From "Versus" to "And"**

Maturing as a leader means realizing that many management choices are not binary trade-offs, but complementary polarities to be managed:

* Giving the team autonomy AND Going deep on critical initiatives
* Prioritizing individual growth AND Driving team performance
* Leading with deep empathy AND Maintaining rigorous objectivity

# **2\. Categorized Directory of Management Frameworks**

To lead effectively, you need structured, repeatable ways to communicate, delegate, make decisions, and grow talent. Below are the core frameworks grouped by their operational category.

## **Category A: Alignment & Communication**

### **1\. The 5 C's Staircase (Building Shared Context)**

One of the biggest causes of team misalignment is a failure to establish shared context. When delegating or kicking off an initiative, walk your team up and down the 5 C's Staircase:

1. Colour: Paint the full picture. Set a crystal-clear intention, establish priority, and define "What does 'done' look like?" What specifically do you expect to see upon completion?
2. Context: Discuss what is happening in surrounding parts of the organization that will influence or be impacted by this decision or project.
3. Connective Tissue: Help people understand the "why." How does this work solve historical problems, support current initiatives, and lay the foundation for future strategy?
4. Cost: Be transparent about trade-offs. Discuss the required time, budget, bandwidth, and opportunity costs. What are we pausing to make room for this?
5. Consequence: Outline the stakes. What is the risk of doing nothing? What unintended downstream consequences must we proactively solve for?

### **2\. The OARB Feedback Framework**

Use the OARB Framework to deliver feedback that is objective, actionable, and clearly tied to your report's self-interest:

* Observation: Describe the specific behavior or words objectively. ("When you say/do \[X\]...")
* Assertion: Describe how that behavior is perceived or how it comes across. ("It can make you seem \[negative perception\]...")
* Repercussion: Explain how this behavior makes their own life or work harder. ("Which isn't ideal because it limits your ability to \[impact/consequence\]...")
* Benefit: Suggest an alternative and explain how it directly helps them achieve their goals. ("If you try \[suggested action\] instead, you’ll find that \[positive benefit\] occurs...")

OARB in Action (Example)

* Observation: "When you say 'yeah, sure' to initiatives during planning meetings but then proceed to execute in a different direction..."
* Assertion: "...it can make you seem untrustworthy. Stakeholders aren't sure if your agreement is genuine or if you're planning to bypass the team decision once you're back at your desk."
* Repercussion: "This means people will start questioning your updates more closely and checking in on your progress constantly, which wastes your time and slows down your autonomy."
* Benefit: "I know it can feel uncomfortable to disagree in real-time. Next time, try saying: 'I have some reservations about this approach and want to take 24 hours to write down my alternative logic so we can compare.' This keeps you from agreeing falsely, asserts your perspective, and protects your time. How does that feel?"

### **3\. S.W.I.M. Communications Framework**

For high-impact, big-moment communications, structure your messages using Caryn Marooney's S.W.I.M. Framework:

4. Situation: Establish the current landscape and reality.
5. What is the problem: Define the friction point, roadblock, or challenge.
6. Impact: Explain what is at stake and why solving this matters right now.
7. Meaning: Define what this means for the company, the team, and individuals long-term.

### **2\. Team Operational Maturity (The Ben-Trey Framework)**

Assess your team's operational systems and work to systematically move them up the maturity ladder:

1. Level 1 — Chaotic: Metrics are nonexistent. Procedures are undocumented and rely entirely on individual heroic efforts. Results vary wildly depending on who is doing the work.
2. Level 2 — Managed: Roles and responsibilities are clearly defined and documented. Tasks are assigned and tracked systematically to completion.
3. Level 3 — Defined: Key metrics are defined, targets are documented, and processes are standard.
4. Level 4 — Measured: Core metrics are actively measured and compared against targets. Conformance to process is tracked, and external feedback loops (e.g., customer satisfaction) are established.
5. Level 5 — Continuous Improvement: Target metric values and procedures are reviewed regularly. The team incorporates both internal retrospectives and external industry best practices to systematically optimize operations.

### **3\. Decisive Alignment: The Inner and Outer Loops**

To keep your team highly aligned and performing, regularly audit your Inner Loop and Outer Loop responsibilities ([David Ulevitch](https://twitter.com/davidu/status/1772264693760963022)):

* The Inner Loop (Weekly Alignment):
  1. Does your team know what is important right now?
  2. Do they know why it is important?
  3. Do they know how they are performing against what is important?
* The Outer Loop (Strategic Health):
  1. Do you have the people needed to win?
  2. Do you have the capital and resources needed to win?
  3. Does what you are building actually matter to the business?

## **Category D: Leadership & Talent Development**

### **1\. The tGROW Coaching Model**

Structure your development-focused 1:1 sessions around the tGROW model:

* Topic: What specific area or challenge do you want to focus on today?
* Goal: What does success look like for this conversation? What do you want to walk away with?
* Reality: What is currently happening? What have you tried, and what are the primary obstacles?
* Options: What are all the possible courses of action? What would you do if you had unlimited resources?
* Will: What specific, actionable steps are you committing to take this week? How can I hold you accountable?

# **3\. The Case for Coaching: Unlocking High Performance**

In world-class athletics, the necessity of a coach is unquestioned. The most gifted tennis players, gymnasts, and runners in the world do not hire coaches because they lack technical knowledge or skill. They hire coaches to obtain objective feedback, push past blind spots, build rigorous self-awareness, and strategically analyze their game.
The same rule applies to organizational leadership. Operating at a high level is a grueling endurance test. To scale yourself and your team, relying on your own instincts is rarely enough.

### **The Support Spectrum**

Understanding the difference between different professional advocates is crucial:

14. A Mentor talks to you — sharing their past experiences and giving advice.
15. A Sponsor talks about you — advocating for your advancement behind closed doors.
16. A Coach talks with you — asking powerful questions to unlock your own latent potential.

### **Self-Coaching: Reflecting on Burnout & Boundaries**

A primary responsibility of leadership is maintaining a sustainable pace of work. Use these diagnostic questions to assess your energy exchange:

17. What is out of balance in your work? What are you giving too much of yourself to without an equal exchange?
18. What boundaries are currently lacking that would protect me from chronic imbalances?
19. Are you resting? Are you letting yourself rest without guilt for the remaining to-do list?
20. Physical health (sleep, movement, recovery) is mental health. Are you protecting your sleep so you can process daily workplace stressors?
